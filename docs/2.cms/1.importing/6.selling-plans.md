---
title: Selling Plans
---

Selling plans are Shopify's purchase options: subscriptions ("deliver every month, 10% off"), prepaid bundles and so on. They're created by whichever app you use (Shopify Subscriptions, Recharge, Seal, Loop, ...), but they're stored on your products in Shopify itself, so the addon reads them the same way whatever the app.

The addon imports the plans, shows them in the control panel and on your site, and adds them to the cart. Managing subscriptions after checkout (pausing, skipping, cancelling) happens in your subscription app's customer portal.

## Enabling

Selling plans aren't imported by default. Add the scopes below to your app, then turn them on:

```env
SHOPIFY_SELLING_PLANS=true
```

Re-import your products (`php artisan shopify:import:all`) and register the selling plan webhooks (`php artisan shopify:webhooks:register`).

#### Scopes

| API | Scope | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Admin | `read_purchase_options` | Importing products' selling plans (the selling plan group webhooks only need `read_products`) |
| Storefront | `unauthenticated_read_selling_plans` | Adding a line to the cart with a selling plan, and reading the cart's plan. In [markets mode](#multi-store-and-markets), also each market's plan prices at import. |

> [!WARNING]
> Turn on `SHOPIFY_SELLING_PLANS` only once your app has the Admin scope. Without it Shopify returns no product data, and every import fails with an "Access denied" error in the [activity log](/cms/sync/activity).

## What's imported

On each **product**:

| Field | |
|---|---|
| `requires_selling_plan` | The product can only be bought on a plan (e.g. subscription only) |
| `selling_plan_groups` | The product's selling plan groups and their plans (see below) |

On each **variant**:

| Field | |
|---|---|
| `selling_plan_ids` | The ids of the plans this variant can be bought on. Variants can be in different groups. |

Each group has an `id`, `name`, `app_id` (the app that created it), `options` and `selling_plans`. Each plan has:

| Key | |
|---|---|
| `id` | The plan's id (`gid://shopify/SellingPlan/...`), as sent to the cart |
| `name`, `description`, `options` | As set up in your subscription app |
| `recurring_deliveries` | Whether the plan delivers more than once |
| `interval`, `interval_count` | How often it delivers, e.g. `MONTH` and `1` |
| `pricing_policies` | A list of `adjustment_type` (`PERCENTAGE`, `FIXED_AMOUNT` or `PRICE`), `adjustment_value` and `after_cycle` (the delivery the policy starts from; empty for the first) |

In the control panel, the plans are shown read-only in the **Subscriptions** section of the product's **Details** tab.

## Keeping plans up to date

Products are re-imported, with their plans, whenever Shopify sends a product webhook. Changing a plan in your subscription app doesn't update the product, so three more webhooks are registered when selling plans are enabled: **Selling Plan Group Create**, **Update** and **Delete** (see [webhooks](/cms/sync/webhooks#webhook-endpoints)). Each re-imports the group's products, and the imported products that had the group, so products removed from a group or a deleted group are updated too.

## Multi-store and markets

- **Unified multi-store**: each store has its own plans. The primary store's are the product's own fields; every store's are kept in `multi_store_selling_plans.{store}` on the product and `multi_store_data.{store}.selling_plan_ids` on each variant. Pass `store` to the [tags](/frontend/getting-started/subscriptions) to use a store's plans.
- **Localized multi-store**: each store's products are separate entries, so they have their own plans.
- **Markets**: plans are the same in every market, but their prices aren't. For each market the import asks the Storefront API for each variant's price on each plan in that market's country (the price checkout will charge), and stores it on the variant:

  ```yaml
  market_data:
    IE:
      price: '25.00'
      selling_plans:
        'gid://shopify/SellingPlan/123':
          price: '22.50'
          compare_at_price: '25.00'
          per_delivery_price: '22.50'
  ```

  The [tags](/frontend/getting-started/subscriptions) and the bundled JS use these prices for every kind of plan. This needs a Storefront token (`SHOPIFY_STOREFRONT_TOKEN`, or `SHOPIFY_STOREFRONT_PRIVATE_TOKEN` to use a [private token](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/usage/authentication#getting-started-with-private-access) on the server), with the `unauthenticated_read_selling_plans` and `unauthenticated_read_product_listings` scopes, and the product must be published to the token's sales channel. It's one Storefront request per market for each product with selling plans.

  If there's no stored price for a market and plan (no token, the product isn't on the channel, or the request failed), prices fall back to working them out from the market's price: percentage discounts are applied, but fixed amount and fixed price plans get no price, as their amounts are in your shop's currency. A failed request never stops the product importing: it's logged as a warning and noted on the import in the [activity log](/cms/sync/activity).

  Plan names don't change per market. If your app puts the discount in the name (Shopify Subscriptions does: "Deliver every 2 months, £3.00 off"), use the tag's [`label`](/frontend/getting-started/subscriptions#listing-plans) on the storefront instead.

## Upgrading

An update script adds the new fields to existing product and variant blueprints. If you manage blueprints in version control, commit the updated blueprints after upgrading.

The product's **Subscriptions** section is only shown in the control panel while `SHOPIFY_SELLING_PLANS` is on. Its fields use the `custom shopifySellingPlansEnabled` [field condition](https://statamic.dev/fields/conditional-fields#custom-logic), which reads the setting, and Statamic hides a section when none of its fields are shown. You can use the same condition on your own fields.
